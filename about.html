Golden, Colorado · · LinkedIn

I spend my time on a narrow question with wide consequences: how do you engineer physical infrastructure so that a cyber attack can't turn into a blackout, a spill, or a shutdown?

For seven years I worked at the National Laboratory of the Rockies — formerly NREL — as a Senior Cybersecurity Researcher and Principal Investigator, a DOE federally funded research and development center. I led the Network and Communication Pillar of Liberty Eclipse, a DOE CESER full-scale national exercise validating grid recovery across highly distributed systems. I architected the ARIES Cyber Range into a high-fidelity environment capable of emulating multi-owner power systems and regional grids, so that defenses could be tested against representative infrastructure instead of simplified test beds.

Alongside that, I've spent a decade teaching. Graduate coursework at the Colorado School of Mines, professional training through the DOE OCIO-funded Cyber Fire Foundry program, and hands-on challenges at DEF CON's ICS Village. I care about the pipeline as much as the technology — the hardest problem in this field is not finding a vulnerability, it's producing engineers who design systems where the vulnerability doesn't matter.
